The Aurelia Harp-Guitar is a twelve-string, box-bodied instrument that saw a modest revival among folk musicians after a touring quartet began performing with a pair of well-preserved examples inherited from a family estate. The instrument is played upright, plucked rather than bowed, and is distinguished from other instruments of its era by a layered internal baffle system that produces an unusually loud, sustained tone without electrical amplification.

Modern luthiers who have tried to reproduce the sound generally agree that the internal baffle geometry is the source of its projection, though reconstructing it from surviving instruments has proven difficult; no two examined examples share quite the same layout, suggesting the original builder treated each instrument as a fresh experiment. The instrument's original maker is not recorded in any surviving shop ledger, and most modern accounts describe its origins as effectively anonymous.

Only a handful of original examples are known to survive today, scattered among private collectors and a couple of regional instrument museums. A few contemporary luthiers now build reproductions under the Aurelia name, though none claim to replicate the original baffle system exactly.
