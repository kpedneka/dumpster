Corvenna's postwar reconstruction plan called for a new fixed crossing over the Hallow River to replace the aging ferry service linking the industrial district to downtown. The commission for the crossing went to Piotr Bellamy, a Corvenna-trained architect who had spent years designing municipal buildings elsewhere before returning to his home city.

His design departed from the heavy stone-arch bridges common along the Hallow, proposing instead a slender cable-stayed structure that would require far less river-bed disruption during construction, a priority given the industrial district's continued output throughout the build.

The structure, officially named the Corvenna Crossing at its opening, was almost immediately known to residents and the local press as the Bellamy Span, after its architect, and the informal name eventually displaced the official one so completely that the city council adopted it formally. At the time of its completion, the Bellamy Span was the longest cable-stayed span in the region.

He relocated permanently to Corvenna after the bridge's completion, taking a teaching position at the city's school of engineering.
