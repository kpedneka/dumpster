A retrospective on Corvenna's transit infrastructure, prepared for the city's transit authority.

The opening of the Bellamy Span marked the end of ferry service between the industrial district and downtown Corvenna, a route that had operated for decades and once carried thousands of passengers daily. The bridge's cable-stayed design later allowed a dedicated tram line to be added along its southern edge, integrating the crossing into the city's streetcar network.

Less well remembered is Bellamy's second Corvenna project, the Corvenna Funicular, a short incline railway connecting the hillside Verrow district to the downtown transit hub below. It addressed a persistent complaint from Verrow residents, who had long faced a steep walk to reach the nearest streetcar stop. The funicular carried a modest but steady number of passengers at its busiest, a fraction of the Bellamy Span's traffic but a vital service for the district it served.

Ridership on the funicular declined as private car ownership rose, and the transit authority eventually decommissioned the line. The incline's original station house still stands, now used as a municipal storage facility.
