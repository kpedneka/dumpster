Elena Voss worked as a ship's navigator, sailing cold trade routes on cargo vessels. She grew frustrated with compass housings that lost their bearing in rough seas, because the needle assembly could not stay level as the deck pitched.

In 1887, after years of sketches and failed prototypes, she completed a working model of a self-leveling mount for the compass bowl. She called her invention the Voss Gimbal. Word of it spread quickly among captains, and demand soon outpaced what she could produce by hand in her own workshop.

Unable to scale production herself, she agreed in 1889 to license the design and sell the manufacturing rights to a shipbuilding-instrument firm. She spent her later years training navigators, largely uncredited outside a small circle of captains.
