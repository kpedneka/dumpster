Meridian Instrument Co. supplied compass housings to merchant fleets. Its early catalog was unremarkable, competing on price against similar workshops.

That changed when Meridian acquired the manufacturing rights to a self-leveling compass mount then being sold in small batches out of a private workshop. The acquisition included the original design drawings and the right to manufacture the mount under its inventor's name. For years, Meridian's catalog listed the product simply as the Voss Gimbal, and it became the firm's best-selling item.

In 1904, following a dispute with the inventor's estate over royalty terms, Meridian rebranded the product as the Steadfast Compass Mount. The internal mechanism was unchanged from the original design. Several longtime customers kept ordering the product by its old name for years afterward, causing confusion in the fulfillment department.

The Steadfast Compass Mount remained in production until Meridian shifted its naval contracts to gyroscopic stabilization systems.
