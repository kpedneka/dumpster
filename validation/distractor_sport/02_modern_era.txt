The regional variation that had splintered Quoribal's rules was eventually resolved when representatives from several coastal cities met to standardize lane dimensions, team size, and scoring zones into a single ruleset, largely restoring Denys Okafor's original pamphlet as the sport's standard with only minor changes to lane length. That same meeting established the Quoribal Federation to govern the sport and organize a regular championship between member cities.

The Federation held its first sanctioned championship, won by a team captained by a dockworker whose family had reportedly played informal versions of the game since its earliest days. The tournament has been held nearly every year since, making it one of the longest continuously governed amateur competitions in the region.

Today the Quoribal Federation oversees leagues in many cities, and the sport retains its open-palm-only rule as a point of pride among players, who regard it as the last surviving link to the dockworkers' game Okafor first wrote down.
