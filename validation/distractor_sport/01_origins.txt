Quoribal traces its origins to dockworkers who began playing an informal game during shift breaks, using a weighted leather disc and a set of chalk-marked lanes drawn on the warehouse floor. The game's name comes from a local dockworkers' term meaning roughly "the marked lane," a reference to the chalked playing surface.

The earliest documented rules, recorded by a schoolteacher named Denys Okafor, describe a game played between two teams of four, each trying to advance the disc down a lane divided into scoring zones while opposing players deflect it using only open palms, never closed fists or any implement. Okafor's pamphlet, written to formalize the dockworkers' game for a planned exhibition match, is considered the founding document of organized Quoribal.

Quoribal spread slowly at first, largely through sailors who had learned the game during layovers and carried informal versions of it to other port cities. Enough regional variation developed over time that the original rules were, in practice, only one of several competing versions being played under the same name.
