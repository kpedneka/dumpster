Talia Renn began trading out of a small harbor town, dealing first in salted fish before turning to the spice trade that would define her career. A shipment of dried kestrel pepper, taken almost by accident as partial payment from a debtor, sold in a single afternoon for far more than she expected.

Over the following years, Renn built a regular route carrying kestrel pepper from highland growers down to merchant houses in a nearby port city, undercutting traders who had never bothered to deal with the highland growers directly. Rival traders grew increasingly resentful of her growing share of the market.

Facing pressure from those rivals and unreliable highland supply routes, Renn organized fellow traders into a formal cooperative, sharing warehouses, ships, and highland contacts under a single banner: The Ashwick Kestrel Traders Guild. The arrangement let member traders weather bad harvest years by pooling stock.

Renn served as the guild's first master until her death, having by then expanded its trade beyond kestrel pepper into several other spice varieties.
