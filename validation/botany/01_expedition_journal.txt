Field journal of Dr. Amara Solis, Continental Botanical Institute expedition.

Day 14. We have made camp at the base of the eastern ridge, behind the schedule the Institute approved before we left the coast. Even so, morale is high; yesterday's specimens included several uncatalogued fern varieties.

Day 19. Today I found what I believe to be an entirely new orchid species, growing in a narrow limestone crevice near the ridge's northern face. The flower is small, pale coral in color, with a fringed lip unlike anything described before. I have collected several specimens and pressed them immediately.

Day 21. I am naming the new orchid, provisionally, the Novaflora Ridge Orchid, after the ridge itself. I intend to write to the Continental Botanical Institute the moment we reach a telegraph station, since the Institute's continued funding may depend on a discovery of this significance. Without the Institute's backing, none of this work would have been possible.

Day 30. We have reached the coast, and a telegram to the Institute has gone out. I suspect the Novaflora Ridge Orchid will outlive all of us in the botanical record, whatever becomes of the Institute itself.
