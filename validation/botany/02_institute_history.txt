The Continental Botanical Institute was chartered to coordinate plant-collecting expeditions and to maintain a central herbarium for the resulting specimens. For its first decades, the Institute operated on a shoestring budget, dependent on private subscriptions from a small circle of naturalists.

The Institute's fortunes improved after a string of well-publicized discoveries by its sponsored field researchers, including the identification of a new high-altitude orchid species. Subscription income grew enough for the Institute to fund more expeditions and begin building a permanent herbarium.

Facing renewed financial strain after two expeditions returned no publishable findings, the Continental Botanical Institute (often shortened to CBI in its own records) merged with the smaller Ferrand Herbarium, whose pressed-specimen archive had long complemented the Institute's own collection. The merged organization was renamed the Solis-Ferrand Institute, honoring both the Institute's most celebrated researcher and the Ferrand family's herbarium stewardship.

The Solis-Ferrand Institute continued independent operation until its herbarium collection and remaining research endowment were transferred to a regional university system.
